# Bangladesh Almond Imports: One of Asia's Highest Duty Rates — And How to Protect Your Margin

Bangladesh's almond market is growing at 15 to 20 percent annually. Premium urban consumers are buying. The Eid gifting market is premiumising. Industrial bakery demand is rising. It looks like an ideal import opportunity — until the duty invoice arrives and the buyer who planned their margin on CIF pricing discovers that Bangladesh applies a tax stack to almonds that qualifies as one of Asia's highest for any food commodity.

The importers who protect their margin in this market are not working around the duty structure. They are the ones who built their business model knowing it from day one.

## The Duty Stack That Most Buyers Discover Too Late

Almonds imported to Bangladesh are classified under HS code 0802.12.00 (shelled) or 0802.11.00 (in-shell), and the duty stack under Bangladesh's National Board of Revenue (NBR) schedule is substantial.

The components of the tax incidence:

**Customs Duty (CD): 25%** applied on the CIF value of the shipment. This is the base layer — standard for nuts under Bangladesh's import tariff schedule.

**Regulatory Duty (RD): 3%** applied on the CIF value. Regulatory duty is NBR's mechanism for additional revenue generation on specific product categories; almonds are included.

**Supplementary Duty (SD): variable**, depending on applicable SRO classification at the time of import. When supplementary duty applies, it adds meaningfully to the cumulative tax base.

**VAT at import: 15%** applied not on CIF alone but on the cumulative base — CIF plus CD plus RD plus SD already applied. This compounding structure is the aspect of Bangladesh's duty calculation that most surprises first-time importers. VAT is applied on a value that already includes customs and regulatory duty, creating an effective VAT rate that exceeds the nominal 15% on the original CIF value.

**Advance Income Tax (AIT): 5%** applied on the cumulative base including all prior duties and VAT.

Total Tax Incidence depending on the specific SRO classification applicable: 64 to 96 percent of CIF value.

## A Worked Example: The Math That Changes How You Build a Margin Model

Take a $5,000 CIF shipment of California Nonpareil shelled almonds — approximately 770 to 850 kilograms depending on count size and moisture content.

Customs Duty at 25%: $1,250. Cumulative base becomes $6,250.
Regulatory Duty at 3% on original CIF: $150. Cumulative base becomes $6,400.
VAT at 15% applied on $6,400: $960. Cumulative base becomes $7,360.
Advance Income Tax at 5% on $7,360: $368.

Total duties on a $5,000 CIF shipment: approximately $2,728.
Total landed cost before port charges and delivery: approximately $7,728.
Effective duty rate on CIF value: 54.6%.

Under higher-end SRO classifications where supplementary duty applies — and the applicable rate can vary based on NBR's periodic SRO revisions — the effective total tax incidence on CIF reaches 96%. The 64 to 96 percent range is not a theoretical spread: it reflects real variation in how almonds are classified under Bangladesh's tariff schedule depending on form, presentation, and applicable SRO at the time of customs declaration.

Buyers who plan their procurement budget on CIF pricing without modelling this duty stack are not building a margin plan. They are building a surprise.

## Why Almonds Still Make Compelling Business Sense at This Duty Level

The duty structure is an argument for planning, not an argument against the category. Bangladesh's almond market makes business sense at this duty level for reasons grounded in the end-market price reality.

California Nonpareil almonds at the current market run approximately $5.50 to $7.00 per kilogram CIF. After applying the duty stack at the mid-range effective rate, total landed cost arrives at approximately $8.50 to $11.00 per kilogram before port handling and inland logistics.

Wholesale pricing in Dhaka's Karwan Bazar commodity market for California almonds runs BDT 1,100 to 1,400 per kilogram — approximately $10 to $13 at current exchange rates. Premium supermarket retail — Shwapno premium tier, Meena Bazar — runs BDT 1,400 to 1,800 per kilogram. The Eid gift pack format — 500 grams in premium presentation — retails at BDT 900 to 1,200 per pack, which at the per-kilogram equivalent represents a significant retail margin uplift over bulk commodity pricing.

The math works. The duty cost is absorbed in the margin between landed cost and retail price, and that margin exists because the premium urban consumer who buys California almonds in Dhaka is not price-sensitive in the same way that edible oil or sugar buyers are. Almonds are a discretionary premium purchase, and discretionary premium purchases carry different price elasticity.

The question is not whether the margin exists. The question is whether you entered the market knowing the full landed cost, or whether you discovered it after the customs invoice arrived and found that your margin model required revision.

## The BFSA Compliance Factor: Why USDA-Inspected California Is the Risk-Adjusted Choice

Beyond the duty structure, almond procurement decisions involve a compliance variable that materially affects clearance timelines and risk exposure.

United States-origin California almonds — specifically USDA-inspected lots — carry mandatory pasteurization documentation. US federal law requires that all California-grown almonds be pasteurized before leaving the United States for any export market. The two approved methods are propylene oxide (PPO) treatment (a fumigant treatment effective against Salmonella) and steam pasteurization (a heat treatment alternative for buyers who prefer non-chemical methods).

USDA-inspected California almonds arrive in Bangladesh with a documentation package that includes the PPO or steam treatment certificate, a Certificate of Analysis covering microbiological testing, and pesticide residue certificates that address Bangladesh Food Safety Authority's Maximum Residue Level requirements. This documentation package satisfies BFSA's import inspection requirements without triggering additional laboratory sampling.

Almonds from alternative origins — Australia, Spain, Iran — may not carry equivalent documentation, and BFSA's response to documentation gaps is port sampling followed by laboratory testing. That testing process adds two to three weeks to clearance. For an Eid-season almond shipment with a retail-delivery deadline, a three-week port hold is not a minor inconvenience. It is a commercial failure.

The USDA-origin premium in almond pricing — California Nonpareil costs more than Australian almonds of comparable kernel size — is partly a compliance risk premium. Buyers who select California origin for Bangladesh import are paying for documentation certainty, not just for the California brand identity.

## The Nonpareil Specification: Why It Is Bangladesh's Preferred Almond Grade

The Nonpareil variety dominates California almond production and Bangladesh's premium almond import market for practical reasons. Nonpareil almonds are thin-skinned, easily blanched, and have a mild flavor profile that makes them versatile across confectionery, bakery, and fresh consumption applications. Their skin releases easily without affecting the kernel, which matters for processors making blanched almond applications.

Count size — expressed as kernels per ounce — determines the application profile. 23/25 count (23 to 25 kernels per ounce) is the large whole almond specification preferred for premium retail and gifting. 32/34 count works for bakery and confectionery applications where per-kernel presentation matters less than volume and cost. Bangladesh institutional buyers in bakery production typically specify 32/34; retail and gifting buyers specify 23/25.

Moisture content is the storage stability variable. California Nonpareil typically delivers at 3.5 to 5% moisture — compatible with Bangladesh's storage environment when proper warehouse humidity control is maintained. Almonds stored above 6% moisture in Bangladesh's climate are susceptible to mold and rancidity acceleration.

## The Importer Capability Gap That Determines Your Actual Experience

Every element described above — duty calculation, USDA documentation, PPO treatment certificates, BFSA compliance, Nonpareil grade specification, moisture management — represents a question about your importer's operational capability before it is a question about your own procurement team's ability.

An importer who cannot tell you the current NBR SRO classification for your almond lot and calculate the exact duty stack does not know your actual landed cost. An importer who has not specified USDA-inspected origin with PPO or steam treatment certificates in their purchase agreement cannot guarantee your BFSA clearance timeline. An importer who sources almonds without verifying moisture content at shipment is creating a warehouse risk you will discover after delivery.

These are not advanced requirements. They are the baseline capabilities that separate importers who can be relied upon in a complex-duty, compliance-sensitive product category from those who manage almond imports the same way they manage bulk commodities where documentation requirements are simpler.
