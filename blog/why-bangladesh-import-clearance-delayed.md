# Why Bangladesh Import Clearance Takes Longer Than Your Supplier Promised

Your Chinese supplier told you the goods would arrive in 30 days. The vessel arrived on schedule. But that was two weeks ago, and your stock hasn't moved. Your importer's updates have gone from "being processed" to "awaiting assessment" to something that sounds increasingly vague. The vessel transit was on time. The delay is entirely onshore — and it is happening inside a customs clearance sequence that most importers have never fully explained to their clients.

Chattogram Port handles approximately 92% of Bangladesh's container imports. Every import that passes through it moves through the same procedural sequence, and there are at least five distinct points in that sequence where a shipment can stall — sometimes for days, sometimes for weeks. Understanding the sequence is the first step to understanding whether your importer is managing it well or managing it poorly.

## The Chattogram Clearance Sequence, Step by Step

**Step 1: Container Arrival at Chattogram Port Authority (CPA)**
The vessel berths, containers are unloaded, and CPA issues an arrival notice. The import clock starts here — free time in the container yard typically runs 3-4 days depending on the shipping line's tariff.

**Step 2: ASYCUDA World e-Filing**
The importer or their CHA files the Bill of Entry (BE) through ASYCUDA World, Bangladesh's customs management system. This filing must include the correct HS classification, declared value, product description, and all supporting documentation — commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, L/C copy, and any applicable regulatory certificates.

A filing delay at this step — even one day — consumes free time in the container yard. An importer who files 2-3 days after container arrival has already consumed most of the free time before customs processing has even begun.

**Step 3: Be-FiRS Risk Channel Assignment**
Once the BE is filed, Bangladesh's automated risk management system — Be-FiRS (Better Facilitation and Risk System) — automatically assigns the shipment to a risk channel. This happens within hours of filing. The channel assignment determines everything that follows.

- **Green channel:** No examination required. Duty payment can proceed immediately. This is the fastest possible outcome.
- **Yellow channel:** Document review required. A customs officer reviews the filing documentation for completeness and consistency. This adds 3-5 working days in typical conditions.
- **Red channel:** Physical examination of the cargo required. A customs officer physically inspects the container contents. This adds 7-14 working days — and the container must be moved to an examination bay, creating additional logistics.

Most importers only find out which channel they've been assigned after they've already been waiting. An in-house customs team monitors the ASYCUDA system and knows channel assignment within hours of filing.

**Step 4: Duty Assessment**
Once the documentation review or physical examination is complete, customs issues the duty assessment — the formal calculation of all applicable duties and taxes (CD, RD, SD, VAT, AIT). If the importer's declared HS classification or declared value differs from customs' determination, an assessment dispute is raised. Resolving a duty dispute adds additional time — potentially weeks if the dispute escalates.

**Step 5: Duty Payment**
After assessment, the importer pays the assessed duties through the treasury. Bank processing times apply. Payment must be confirmed before the next step.

**Step 6: CFS/Yard Release and Delivery Order**
After duty payment confirmation, customs issues the Out-of-Charge (OOC) notice. The importer then collects the Delivery Order from the shipping line and presents it at the container freight station or yard for cargo release. Even at this final stage, CPA yard congestion can add 1-2 days to physical container collection.

## Where Delays Actually Accumulate

Understanding the sequence helps identify the specific delay triggers that turn a 5-day clearance into a 21-day ordeal.

### Be-FiRS Red Channel Assignment

The single largest source of unpredictable delay is Be-FiRS red channel assignment. The system applies risk scores based on multiple factors: HS code, declared value relative to reference prices, the importer's compliance history, the supplier's trade profile, and product category risk levels.

New importers, new suppliers, new product categories, and products with high duty evasion histories all carry elevated risk scores. An importer who handles a wide variety of products without building a clean Be-FiRS compliance history accumulates risk flags. The result is that their shipments consistently land in yellow or red channels while competitors with clean profiles move through green.

Building a green-channel-compatible compliance profile takes years of consistent, accurate filing. It cannot be manufactured in a single shipment.

### BFSA Inspection Hold

For food product imports — including edible oils, dairy products, pulses, spices, and processed foods — BFSA inspection is an additional layer beyond the Be-FiRS channel assignment. BFSA inspection can run concurrently with customs examination in theory, but in practice, the BFSA officer's availability and the inspection queue determine the timeline. A BFSA hold that arrives without prior approval documentation can extend the total clearance to 3-4 weeks.

### Duty Assessment Disputes

When an importer's declared HS code or value differs from customs' reference data, the assessment dispute process begins. Customs may refer the matter to the valuation officer, request additional documentation, or issue a provisional assessment pending review. Each step in the dispute process adds time — and accumulates port detention charges throughout.

The prevention is accurate HS classification and realistic value declaration, applied consistently across every shipment to build a predictable assessment pattern.

### Bank Payment Processing Lag

After duty assessment, the payment step is not instantaneous. AD bank processing, treasury confirmation, and customs system update can add 1-3 working days even for an importer who moves immediately. An importer who takes 24 hours to initiate payment after receiving the assessment has extended the timeline unnecessarily.

### CPA Container Yard Congestion

Chattogram Port runs at high utilization throughout the year, but congestion peaks are structural and predictable. Pre-Ramadan booking surge (typically June-August) and Ramadan clearance concentration (typically March-April) create container yard backlogs that affect even well-managed shipments. During peak congestion, even a green-channel clearance may see 2-4 additional days at the physical collection stage.

An importer who understands seasonal congestion patterns plans arrival timing accordingly — avoiding peak congestion windows where possible for non-urgent shipments, and building extra buffer into timelines for those that can't avoid them.

## Realistic Clearance Timelines

Best case (green channel, complete documentation, no disputes, no BFSA hold): **2-3 working days** from filing to cargo release.

Realistic average (yellow channel, standard documentation, no disputes): **5-8 working days** from filing to cargo release.

Yellow channel with a BFSA food inspection: **10-15 working days.**

Red channel with duty assessment dispute: **14-21+ working days** — and this is where port detention charges can reach USD 1,500-4,000 for a standard 20-foot container.

The gap between best-case and worst-case is not random. It is the result of specific, manageable decisions made at the pre-shipment stage — classification accuracy, regulatory pre-compliance, documentation preparation, and filing timing. An importer who manages these variables systematically delivers clearance at the low end of the range, consistently. One who doesn't is delivering the worst-case scenario on a regular basis and calling it normal.

## What "In-House Customs" Actually Means for Your Timeline

The practical difference between an importer with in-house customs staff and one using an outsourced CHA shows up most clearly in the Be-FiRS monitoring step. An in-house team watches the ASYCUDA system in real time. They know within hours if a shipment is assigned to red channel. They can immediately initiate the examination scheduling process, prepare supplementary documentation, and be present at the physical examination — all of which compress the red-channel timeline.

An outsourced CHA finds out about red channel assignment when they next check the system — which may be the following day. They then need to fit your container's examination into their schedule alongside all their other clients. The structural prioritization works against you when your cargo is managed as a transaction rather than as a client relationship.
