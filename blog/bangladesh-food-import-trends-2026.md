# Bangladesh Food Import Trends 2026: Which Commodities Are Surging, Which Are Vulnerable, and What B2B Buyers Should Know

Bangladesh imports more than $8 billion worth of food and agricultural products every year. Behind that number is a procurement story that most B2B buyers are navigating with incomplete data, informal broker relationships, and supply chains that were built for a market that no longer exists.

## The Structural Foundation: Why Bangladesh's Food Import Dependence Is Permanent

Before examining what is changing, it helps to understand why Bangladesh's food import scale is not a temporary condition. The country's food import dependence is structural — 170 million people, limited arable land per capita relative to population size, and rising incomes driving processed food consumption faster than domestic agricultural production can match.

NBR and EPB data put total food and agricultural imports in the $8-10 billion annual range, with the composition shifting meaningfully over the past five years. The companies that build procurement relationships and supply chain capabilities aligned with where the market is heading — not where it was — will operate from structural advantage. The rest will spend more time chasing spot availability and managing clearance surprises.

These are the five trends shaping Bangladesh's food import market in 2026 and into 2027.

## Trend 1 — Edible Oils: The Dominant Category Is Being Restructured by Geopolitics

Edible oil — soyabean, palm, sunflower, and other — is Bangladesh's single largest food import category by value: approximately $1.5 to $2 billion per year at current volumes. Volume runs approximately 2 to 2.5 million metric tons annually. This is not a niche category; it is the backbone of Bangladesh's food processing industry, household cooking, and the HRI sector.

The composition of that $2 billion is changing, and the change was triggered by geopolitics rather than consumer preference.

Palm oil from Malaysia and Indonesia dominates by volume — cheapest landed cost, widest availability. Soyabean oil from Argentina and Brazil holds the quality mid-tier: better lipid profile than palm, broader application in food manufacturing. Sunflower oil from Ukraine and Argentina is the health-premium segment — and it has grown from approximately 5% of the edible oil import mix five years ago to more than 12% market share today.

The Ukraine war in 2022 disrupted the global sunflower oil supply chain profoundly. Ukraine is the world's largest sunflower oil exporter. When Ukrainian export logistics collapsed in early 2022, global sunflower oil prices spiked by more than 60%. Bangladeshi importers who had built single-source relationships with Ukrainian suppliers faced a supply gap with no rapid alternative.

The recovery from that shock permanently restructured smart importers' sourcing strategies. Dual-origin sourcing — Ukraine and Argentina — is now standard practice for sunflower oil importers who learned from 2022. Argentina produces high-oleic sunflower oil varieties with longer shelf life, and Argentine export logistics are stable. Importers who built Argentine relationships in 2022 and 2023 now hold a supply security advantage that single-source competitors do not.

## Trend 2 — Pulses: Forced Diversification Created New Supply Chains and New Winners

India's multiple pulse export bans between 2016 and 2023 constitute the most significant structural disruption in Bangladesh's agricultural import history — and they produced a supply chain transformation that continues to reshape the market today.

Bangladesh historically sourced most of its pulse imports from India, predominantly due to geographic proximity and established broker networks. India's bans — covering red lentils, yellow lentils, chickpeas, and other pulses at various points — forced Bangladeshi importers to develop alternative supply chains rapidly, under price pressure, with unfamiliar counterparties in distant markets.

The result: Canadian red lentils are now Bangladesh's preferred specification for the category. Canadian-origin red lentils command quality consistency — machine-cleaned, colour-sorted, reliable moisture content — that Indian origin could not guarantee at volume. Australian Kabuli chickpeas at 9mm grade are the benchmark for Bangladesh's chickpea market. Australian and Canadian split peas now anchor the pulse import portfolio.

The diversification improved supply security meaningfully. No single origin controls access to Bangladesh's pulse market anymore. But the transition exposed a capability gap: importers who had not built relationships with Canadian and Australian suppliers before the bans accelerated found themselves buying through brokers at premiums, receiving inconsistent quality, and managing BFSA documentation requirements for unfamiliar origins.

The importers who built direct relationships with Canadian and Australian exporters from 2019 onward now operate with better pricing, better quality control, and faster access to favoured specifications when seasonal supply tightens.

## Trend 3 — Premium Nuts: Almonds and Cashews Are Growing Fast, and the Data Is Consistent

Bangladesh's almond import has grown 15 to 20 percent annually for the past five years. This is not a one-year anomaly — it is a sustained trend driven by three converging factors.

Urban health consciousness is reshaping the protein and snack categories. The "10 almonds a day" nutritional message has penetrated educated urban consumer segments. Dhaka's premium grocery retail sector — Shwapno premium, Meena Bazar, Agora — has expanded almond shelf space and variety significantly over the past three years.

Eid gifting culture creates a concentrated premium demand event. Almonds in presentation tins and gift packs are now standard offerings in the corporate gifting market and the premium household Eid exchange. The gift pack format captures retail margin that bulk commodity trading does not.

Bakery and confectionery growth is creating industrial demand. Bangladesh's bakery sector is expanding and professionalising. Almond-based products — almond barfi, almond paste, almond flour applications — are entering product lines that were previously limited by reliable raw material access.

At a Total Tax Incidence of 64 to 96 percent, almonds are an expensive import by any regional comparison. But the premium urban consumer is paying the end retail price, which means the importer who operates efficiently within the duty structure and maintains consistent supply access to USDA-inspected California Nonpareil almonds holds a position in a growing premium market.

## Trend 4 — Dates: The Ramadan Market Is Premiumising at Scale

Bangladesh's date import runs approximately 100,000 to 150,000 metric tons per year, overwhelmingly concentrated in the Ramadan period. Within that volume, the most significant commercial trend is the premiumisation of the date category.

Commodity dates — Egyptian, Iranian, Pakistani Khajur — dominated Bangladesh's market for decades at low price points. The premium tier was small, served by a handful of specialist importers catering to high-income households.

That has changed materially. Medjool dates from Jordan now command three to five times the price of commodity dates, and the market for them is growing, not contracting. Jordan-origin Medjool — large, moist, naturally sweet — has developed an urban brand identity that commodity dates cannot replicate.

Saudi Ajwa dates command even more. Ajwa's significance in Islamic tradition creates demand among religiously observant consumers who specifically seek it as a distinct product, not a date substitute. Saudi-origin Ajwa, properly documented and handled, is a premium product with inelastic demand among its target consumer.

Importers who established direct relationships with Jordanian and Saudi exporters three to five years ago built market positions that now command the premium end of Bangladesh's fastest-growing food import seasonal event. The barriers to entry are relationship-based: Jordanian and Saudi exporters with consistent quality allocate their premium lots to established buyers first.

## Trend 5 — Asian Condiments: The HRI Growth Engine

Bangladesh's restaurant sector has grown more than 200 percent since 2015, with a particular concentration in Dhaka's evolving food-service landscape. Chinese, Thai, Japanese, and fusion restaurant concepts have moved from novelty to mainstream in Dhaka's commercial dining scene, and that sector's professionalization is driving demand for professional-grade imported condiments.

Soy sauce, oyster sauce, fish sauce, and chili oil imports are growing 15 to 20 percent annually. The market is stratifying by brand quality and buyer segment in ways that mirror more mature Asian food-service markets.

Haitian Seasoning from China serves the budget HRI and production cooking segment: functional, high-volume, price-driven procurement. Lee Kum Kee from Hong Kong serves mid-tier restaurants and food manufacturers who want consistent brand recognition and quality in their supply chain. Kikkoman from Japan serves premium restaurants where provenance is part of the product story.

Each tier has a different importer profile. Budget condiments move through high-volume commodity trading. Premium condiments require an importer who can manage smaller lot sizes, brand-specific documentation, and quality-sensitive institutional buyers.

## The Common Thread Across All Five Trends

Every trend described above rewards importers with supply chain depth, origin relationships, and documentation capability over those who compete on price alone. Edible oil buyers who understand dual-origin sourcing. Pulse importers with Canadian and Australian relationships. Nut importers who navigate the duty structure with precision. Date importers with access to Jordan and Saudi supply. Condiment importers who manage brand complexity.

The commodity trading model — buy cheap, clear fast, deliver to whoever calls — still functions at the volume end. But the institutional buyer market, the premium segment, and the food manufacturer procurement desk have moved past it.
