# India Motorcycle Parts for Bangladesh: The SAFTA Duty Advantage Your Current Supplier Isn't Capturing

There is a legal, available duty reduction on India-origin motorcycle parts entering Bangladesh. It can reduce the total tax incidence on a shipment by 25 to 35 percentage points. The savings on a $100,000 annual parts import run to $50,000 or more. Most importers operating through informal channels are not capturing it — and the reasons why reveal a great deal about the structural cost of informal sourcing.

## What SAFTA Means for Motorcycle Parts

The South Asia Free Trade Agreement grants qualifying India-origin goods preferential duty treatment on entry to Bangladesh. For motorcycle parts classified under HS Chapter 8714, the SAFTA preferential customs duty rate is 0 to 5 percent, depending on the specific subheading. Bangladesh's Most Favoured Nation rate for the same products, applied to imports from countries without a preferential trade arrangement, sits at 25 percent or higher before additional levies are applied.

The full tax incidence on a motorcycle parts import is not the customs duty alone. The applicable charges stack: customs duty, regulatory duty, advance tax, supplementary duty where applicable, and VAT assessed on the cumulative base including duties paid. Under MFN rates, the total tax incidence on motorcycle parts commonly reaches 50 to 60 percent of CIF value. Under SAFTA preferential rates, the same stack, applied to a 3 percent customs duty base, produces a total tax incidence in the range of 22 to 25 percent.

The practical consequence: on a $10,000 CIF shipment of motorcycle body panels, the duty difference between MFN and SAFTA treatment is $3,000 to $4,000. On a $100,000 annual parts program, the SAFTA advantage is $30,000 to $40,000 in direct duty savings — a permanent reduction in landed cost, not a one-time event.

## Why Most Informal Importers Don't Capture It

The SAFTA preferential rate is not automatic. It requires specific documentation and formal import procedures. Each of these requirements is incompatible with informal sourcing channels.

**The Certificate of Origin requirement** is the primary gating condition. To claim SAFTA preferential treatment at Bangladesh customs, the importer must present a Certificate of Origin in Form D for the specific shipment. Form D must be issued by an approved Indian authority — Export Promotion Councils, the Federation of Indian Export Organisations (FIEO), or another designated issuing body. The Certificate must identify the exporter, the consignee, the goods by HS code, and certify that the goods meet SAFTA rules of origin requirements.

Informal importers sourcing through Benapole by luggage trade have no Form D. Their goods have not been declared for export by the Indian supplier. No issuing authority has examined the shipment. The Certificate of Origin is not a document that can be obtained after the fact or manufactured independently — it is issued by an authorised Indian body at the time of export, based on verified documentation from the manufacturer.

**The importer registration requirement** is the second condition. SAFTA benefit claims at NBR require the Bangladesh importer to hold a valid Import Registration Certificate. An IRC requires a trade licence, TIN, VAT registration, and a bank account capable of handling L/C or telegraphic transfer payments. Most informal traders operating through luggage trade or personal courier do not hold IRCs — and the ones who do must still import through documented channels to apply the preferential rate.

**The HS code declaration requirement** closes the loop. SAFTA preferential treatment applies to specific HS codes. A Form D citing one HS code cannot be used to claim benefit on a different classification. If the importer declares goods under an incorrect HS heading — whether through error or to minimise duty — the SAFTA claim on those goods is invalid. Correct HS classification requires knowledge of the Bangladesh Customs Tariff and experience with how NBR classifies specific motorcycle parts. A misclassification that appears to save duty on one line may void the preferential benefit entirely.

The combination of these requirements means that capturing SAFTA benefit on India-origin motorcycle parts demands formal import infrastructure: an IRC, an Indian supplier with export documentation systems, a Certificate of Origin from an authorised issuing body, and correct HS classification at the Bangladesh customs declaration stage. These are not minor administrative requirements. They are the price of accessing a preferential rate that is, once accessed, significant and repeatable.

## The Ludhiana Supply Chain and Documentation

Ludhiana, Punjab is India's primary motorcycle parts manufacturing cluster — informally called the Sheffield of India. The city hosts more than 5,000 auto-component manufacturers, ranging from large Tier-1 suppliers to Hero and Bajaj assembly plants to specialised SMEs producing specific components. This manufacturing depth means that virtually every part category for India-dominant motorcycle models — fuel tanks, body panels, brake assemblies, engine components — can be sourced from Ludhiana with manufacturer certification.

Crucially, Ludhiana's established export manufacturers have the documentation infrastructure that SAFTA requires. Their quality management systems are ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 certified. They participate in India's export promotion network, meaning their shipments can obtain Form D from relevant Export Promotion Councils. Their invoices, packing lists, and shipping documents are structured to satisfy Bangladesh customs documentation requirements. They have done this before, repeatedly, for export-oriented buyers.

This is not true of informal supply sources. A small uncertified manufacturer supplying a luggage trader in Amritsar does not have export promotion council membership. They do not issue COAs. Their production records are not structured to satisfy a certificate of origin application. The documentation infrastructure does not exist because they have never needed it — their entire business model bypasses formal export channels.

## The Compound Effect on Landed Cost

Distributors who currently source informally are typically not aware that they are paying MFN duty rates, because informal sourcing often involves partial or complete duty evasion rather than formal duty payment. The apparent cost advantage of informal sourcing is, in significant part, the cost of unpaid taxes — which creates a risk of seizure, penalty, and regulatory scrutiny rather than a sustainable cost structure.

A distributor who transitions to formal import through a supplier who captures SAFTA preferential rates is not simply choosing to pay more tax for the privilege of documentation. They are making a different trade: documented, insurable, warranty-backed supply at a total landed cost that, at volume, is competitive with or lower than the true all-in cost of informal sourcing when defect rates, missing warranty, and logistics inefficiency are properly accounted for.

The SAFTA advantage compounds with volume. Per-unit customs duty savings on a 500-unit body panel order are the same as on a 50-unit order in percentage terms — but the absolute saving is ten times larger. A distributor who consolidates their parts purchasing into quarterly formal import orders is capturing the duty advantage at scale, while also reducing per-unit freight costs and improving specification consistency.

## What to Ask Your Current Supplier

The simplest test of whether your current motorcycle parts supplier is capturing SAFTA benefit is to ask for the Certificate of Origin in Form D for your last shipment. If they cannot produce it, the shipment entered Bangladesh either under MFN duty rates — meaning you paid 25+ percent more duty than necessary — or without full duty payment, meaning the import is undocumented and uninsurable.

Either outcome is a cost that a formal import partnership can eliminate.
