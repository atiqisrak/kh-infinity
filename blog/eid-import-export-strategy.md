Bangladesh has two Eids. Each is the largest consumption event of the year in its respective category. Together, they structure the import and export calendar for virtually every product category that KHI handles — food commodities, handicrafts, potatoes, electronics, motorcycle parts.

Importers and exporters who understand the Eid procurement calendar book at the right time and capture the margin. Those who don't find themselves buying at peak prices, shipping against congested port schedules, and delivering into a market that has already been served by the importers who planned ahead.

## Eid ul Fitr: The Food Commodity Event

Eid ul Fitr follows Ramadan — the month of fasting and collective consumption. The Ramadan-Eid ul Fitr window is Bangladesh's peak demand period for edible oils, sugar, dates, spices, condensed milk, chickpeas, and processed foods.

**The demand curve matters more than the event date.** Consumption does not spike on Eid day — it builds through Ramadan and peaks in the final two weeks of fasting. Importers who wait for Ramadan to begin before placing orders are already 3-4 months late. The supply chain — supplier booking, production, vessel transit, Chattogram clearance, inland distribution — requires that cargo arrive at the distribution point before Ramadan begins.

Working backward from a typical late-March Eid ul Fitr:

- **Mid-November:** Supplier booking, contract finalization, LC opening
- **December:** Production and loading for bulk commodities
- **January (early):** Vessel departure from origin ports
- **January-February:** Arrival at Chattogram, customs clearance
- **February:** Inland distribution to wholesalers and retailers
- **March (Ramadan):** Product in market, sales at peak demand

Importers who book in January for a March Eid are not buying Ramadan stock — they are paying a 15-25% spot premium to purchase cargo that other importers have already locked in at forward prices.

**The categories where the premium is highest:**

*Dates:* Medjool dates from Morocco and Palestine, Ajwa from Saudi Arabia, Deglet Noor from Tunisia. Premium Medjool dates with strong provenance documentation see 30-40% price appreciation from October booking price to March arrival price. Importers who commit early, with quality-verified sourcing, can offer consistent supply at stable price — a significant advantage with institutional buyers (supermarket chains, premium gift basket distributors).

*Edible oils:* Sunflower oil and soyabean oil for consumer-pack Ramadan promotions. Demand for smaller consumer SKUs increases substantially. Importers who only offer bulk 200L drums miss the consumer-pack segment.

*Spices and condiments:* Cumin, coriander, chili — Ramadan cooking spikes demand for whole and ground spices across the Bangladesh consumer market.

## Eid ul Adha: The Export Event

Eid ul Adha — the Festival of Sacrifice — is different. It is the larger commercial event for the Gulf diaspora market and the occasion that drives Bangladesh's potato and vegetable export calendar into GCC markets.

Bangladesh's Munshiganj potato harvest cycle aligns with a March-April collection and an April-May export window — positioning for delivery into GCC markets ahead of Eid ul Adha, which typically falls in June-July on the Gregorian calendar. The 10+ million South Asian diaspora residents in the Gulf are a primary demand driver for Bangladeshi produce at this time.

**The export preparation window opens in January.** Exporters who want to ship Munshiganj potatoes to UAE, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia in May need to:

- Confirm buyer purchase orders and pricing by January-February
- Arrange phytosanitary certification from the Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) in advance
- Book reefer container slots well ahead — cold chain capacity from Chattogram to Gulf ports is limited and fills quickly as the season approaches
- Confirm GCC import standards compliance (food safety, grading, maximum residue limits for pesticides)

Exporters who begin this process in April, after the harvest is in, find reefer container availability constrained, phytosanitary inspection backlogs at DAE, and buyers who have already sourced from Egypt or India.

**Handicrafts and textiles for Eid gifting:** The Eid gift market in Bangladesh — premium Jamdani sarees, Nakshi Kantha, handwoven textiles — also sees strong demand from the Gulf NRB (Non-Resident Bangladeshi) community. Exporters serving this segment need reliable freight partnerships with the Gulf and consistent product quality for repeat buyer relationships.

## The Motorcycle Parts Calendar

Bangladesh's motorcycle parts import demand has a secondary Eid seasonality. Workshop volumes increase before both Eids as motorcycle owners — particularly in rural and semi-urban areas — prepare vehicles for travel to home villages (the Bangladesh practice of returning to origins for Eid). Brake assemblies, cables, fuel tanks, and body panels see elevated replacement demand in the 6-8 weeks before Eid ul Fitr and Eid ul Adha.

Motorcycle parts importers who plan for this cycle — ensuring stock is cleared through customs and in warehouse 6-8 weeks before Eid — capture the pre-Eid workshop demand spike. Those who are still waiting for containers at the Eid window miss it.

## The Electronics Replenishment Window

Eid is Bangladesh's primary gifting season for consumer electronics — smartphones, accessories, audio products. The formal import market sees elevated demand from distributors and retailers who stock up on accessories (charging cables, power banks, earphones, phone cases) for the Eid retail spike.

For electronics importers, the procurement window for Eid ul Fitr is September-December (cargo arriving January-February for market entry before Ramadan). For Eid ul Adha, it is March-May (cargo arriving May-June for pre-Eid retail stocking).

## The Compounding Effect of Getting This Wrong

The importers who respond to Eid demand rather than planning for it face a compounding penalty:

**They pay spot prices.** Commodity markets price expected demand in advance. The importer booking in October pays the forward price; the importer booking in February pays the spot + scarcity premium.

**They compete for constrained capacity.** Vessel space, reefer containers, and port capacity at Chattogram all tighten as Eid approaches. Late bookings pay freight premiums or accept longer transit routing.

**They deliver late.** A shipment that clears Chattogram three weeks into Ramadan misses the peak demand window and arrives into a market that has already been served. The product sells, but at the market clearing price rather than the seasonal premium.

The importers who consistently win on seasonal products — dates, edible oils, spices, potatoes — are not smarter about procurement. They are earlier. The calendar advantage compounds: early buyers build supplier relationships, secure preferred pricing, and develop the documentation infrastructure for faster clearances — creating a structural advantage that widens each year.

## Planning Backward from the Eid Calendar

A practical planning framework for Eid-sensitive categories:

**Eid ul Fitr (assume March-April):**
- July-August: Assess previous Eid season performance, identify product gaps
- September-October: Begin supplier discussions, request pricing and availability
- October-November: Commit purchase orders, open LCs
- November-January: Production and transit period
- February: Chattogram clearance, inland distribution
- March: In-market for Ramadan demand

**Eid ul Adha (assume June-July):**
- January-February: Confirm buyer orders for export products, book reefer capacity
- March-April: Harvest/collection, phytosanitary inspection, export documentation
- April-May: Vessel departure from Chattogram, transit to Gulf
- May-June: Arrival and market entry in GCC

The importer who builds this calendar into their annual planning cycle stops reacting to Eid and starts profiting from it.
