Bangladesh is known globally as a garment export powerhouse. The RMG (Ready-Made Garments) sector dominates export revenue, dominates policy attention, and dominates the institutional support infrastructure around export promotion. It also dominates the awareness of what government export incentives exist.

The result is that a substantial set of export incentives — cash assistance programs worth 10-20% of FOB value — go largely unclaimed by non-RMG exporters. Agricultural products, handicrafts, leather goods, furniture, processed foods: each of these sectors has access to government cash assistance schemes through the Export Promotion Bureau (EPB). Most exporters in these categories have never filed a claim.

## What EPB Cash Assistance Actually Is

The Bangladesh government's export cash assistance program provides a direct cash payment to exporters, calculated as a percentage of the FOB (Free on Board) value of their export shipment. The payment is made by the EPB (and in some cases directly by the Bangladesh Bank) after the export is completed and documented.

Unlike a tax credit or a duty drawback, cash assistance is a real cash transfer — the exporter receives a payment from the government in recognition of the export earnings brought into Bangladesh. The program is funded from national budget allocations specifically targeting export diversification away from RMG.

## Rates for Key Non-RMG Categories

Under the current Export Policy Order (with rates reviewed annually in the national budget):

**Potatoes and vegetables:** 15-20% cash incentive on FOB value. Bangladesh potato exports to the Gulf and Middle East qualify, provided phytosanitary documentation and minimum grade requirements are met.

**Processed agro-products and food items:** 20% on FOB value for processed or semi-processed agricultural products with significant local value addition. Eligible products include processed fruits and vegetables, dehydrated foods, and certain packaged food categories.

**Handicrafts, jute products, and cottage industry goods:** 15% on FOB value. Includes Nakshi Kantha, Jamdani-category textiles, bamboo and cane products, and other registered handicraft items.

**Light engineering products:** 15% on FOB, including certain agricultural implements, spare parts, and fabricated metal goods manufactured in Bangladesh.

**Leather and leather goods (non-footwear):** 10-15% depending on value addition level.

On a $200,000 FOB shipment of potatoes to the UAE, a 20% cash incentive represents $40,000 in government cash payment. This is not a marginal benefit — it is the difference between a marginally profitable and a significantly profitable export program.

## Why Most Non-RMG Exporters Miss It

**They don't know it applies to them.** The EPB's communication, industry events, and published materials are heavily oriented toward the garment sector. Agricultural exporters, handicraft exporters, and light engineering exporters frequently operate without awareness that they are eligible.

**The documentation requirement is significant.** To claim cash assistance, the exporter must present: the Export Registration Certificate (ERC), the Bill of Lading or airway bill, the customs export declaration (EXP form), the bank's Foreign Exchange (FE) statement confirming export proceeds received in Bangladesh, and in some categories, a pre-shipment inspection certificate. Assembling this documentation set requires functional export documentation management. Exporters who handle their own export paperwork informally — without a dedicated C&F agent and proper file management — frequently cannot produce the complete set.

**The claims process has a time window.** EPB cash assistance claims must be filed within a specified period after the export. Exporters who are not actively managing their export documentation often miss the window and lose the benefit permanently for that shipment.

**The ERC requirement filters out informal exporters.** Only exporters with a valid Export Registration Certificate from the EPB are eligible. Many small agricultural exporters — particularly in the potato and vegetable sector — operate without an ERC, which bars them from any government incentive program.

## The Bangladesh Bank Foreign Exchange Connection

A critical requirement that many exporters underestimate: the export proceeds must be received through the banking system and reported as foreign exchange earnings. Bangladesh Bank regulations require that export proceeds be repatriated within a specified timeframe (currently four months for most export categories) and reported through the exporter's authorized dealer bank.

Exporters who receive payment informally — cash, informal hundi, or delayed transfer arrangements with foreign buyers — cannot document the foreign exchange receipt required for the cash assistance claim. The incentive program effectively rewards formal, documented export operations and provides zero benefit to informal exporters even when the physical export occurred.

This is not a minor technicality. For small exporters whose buyers prefer informal settlement, the choice between informal payment and access to a 15-20% government incentive is a meaningful business decision.

## What a Compliant Export Operation Looks Like

To access EPB incentives consistently, an exporter needs:

1. **Valid ERC** — Export Registration Certificate from EPB, updated and in current status
2. **C&F agent at export port** — Professional customs clearance generating the EXP form and Bill of Lading
3. **Authorized dealer bank relationship** — A bank with foreign exchange authorization that can handle export proceeds and generate the required FE documentation
4. **Pre-shipment inspection** (where required) — Particularly for agro-products going to GCC markets, a licensed inspection agency certificate is required both by the importing country and by the EPB for incentive purposes
5. **Claims filing system** — A process for assembling and filing the complete documentation set within the claims window

Each of these requirements is individually manageable. Together, they define a level of operational formalization that many small exporters have not reached — which is precisely why the money sits unclaimed.

## The Math for Potato and Handicraft Exporters

Bangladesh exported approximately $80-100 million in potatoes and vegetables in recent years, with significant room to grow given Gulf diaspora demand and competitive pricing relative to Indian and Egyptian alternatives. At a 20% incentive rate, every $1 million of documented potato exports generates $200,000 in government cash assistance.

For handicraft exporters — Jamdani weavers, Nakshi Kantha producers, jute goods manufacturers — the 15% rate on documented exports means that building a compliant export operation pays for itself quickly in incentive recovery.

The exporters capturing these incentives are not special cases. They are businesses that invested in operational documentation infrastructure — and that investment pays ongoing dividends on every export shipment.
